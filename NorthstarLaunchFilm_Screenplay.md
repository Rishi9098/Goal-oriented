# NORTHSTAR
### "One System. Every Number. One True Answer."
**Cinematic Product Launch Film — Production Screenplay**

**Format:** Single continuous film, 8 Acts, 26 Scenes
**Target Runtime:** 7:30 (450 seconds)
**Intended Use:** AI video generation (Google Flow) + voice-over recording + motion design brief
**Tone Reference:** Apple WWDC keynote films, Stripe Sessions, Linear launch films, OpenAI DevDay, Arc Browser introduction
**Accuracy Statement:** every feature shown in Acts 1–6 is implemented and verified against the Northstar codebase (FastAPI/PostgreSQL/SQLAlchemy backend, React frontend, 17 life event handlers, live-computed recommendations, 673-test backend suite). Act 7 is explicitly and visually separated as unreleased vision work. Nothing in Act 7 is depicted as shipped.

---

Each scene contains all production fields in a fixed order: **Scene / Duration / Narration / Visual Description / Camera Direction / Animation / Background / Transitions / On-Screen Text / Emotional Goal / Sound Effects / Music Style.** After all 26 scenes, this document consolidates: the full Voice-Over Script, Shot List, Scene Timing Table, Animation Notes, Music Suggestions, Camera Direction Glossary, Visual Effects List, Transition List, Subtitle Script (SRT-style), and Final Runtime.

# ACT 1 — THE PROBLEM
*(0:00 – 1:00 · 60 seconds · 4 scenes)*

## SCENE 1
**Duration:** 0:00–0:12 (12s)

**Narration:**
> "Somewhere right now, someone is trying to answer a simple question."

**Visual Description:** Pure black frame. A single soft point of white light breathes in the center of the screen — slow pulse, like a heartbeat, like a cursor blinking in an empty field. No UI, no branding yet. The light is the only thing alive in the frame.

**Camera Direction:** Static locked-off shot, dead center framing. No camera movement. Let the silence and the pulse do the work.

**Animation:** The point of light scales from 2px to 6px and back over a 2-second breathing cycle, three full cycles across the scene.

**Background:** Absolute black (#000000).

**Transitions:** Hard cut in from black (film open). Cross-dissolve out to Scene 2.

**On-Screen Text:** None.

**Emotional Goal:** Stillness. Anticipation. The feeling of a question waiting to be asked.

**Sound Effects:** A single low sub-bass heartbeat pulse, synced to the light's breathing rhythm. Room tone underneath — near silence.

**Music Style:** No score yet — only the heartbeat pulse as the seed of the eventual score's tempo.

## SCENE 2
**Duration:** 0:12–0:30 (18s)

**Narration:**
> "'Am I actually going to be okay?' Not someday. Not in theory. Right now, with the money I actually have."

**Visual Description:** The point of light expands into a cluttered desktop — dozens of browser tabs, a banking app, a budgeting spreadsheet, a retirement calculator PDF, a sticky note that reads "check this later," a second monitor with three more tabs. Every window shows a different number for the same "net worth" figure. The clutter is realistic, not cartoonish — this is what an actual overwhelmed desktop looks like.

**Camera Direction:** Slow push-in on the desktop, starting wide (establishing the chaos) and tightening toward the three conflicting net-worth numbers, each in a different app, none of them agreeing.

**Animation:** Windows appear in a rapid stagger (80ms apart), each landing with a subtle drop-shadow snap. The three conflicting numbers pulse red-outline briefly to draw the eye to the contradiction.

**Background:** A muted, slightly desaturated desktop wallpaper — cool gray-blue, deliberately unglamorous.

**Transitions:** Cross-dissolve in from Scene 1's point of light (the light becomes the cursor on this desktop). Hard cut out to Scene 3.

**On-Screen Text:** None — let the three conflicting numbers speak for themselves.

**Emotional Goal:** Recognition. "I've felt exactly this."

**Sound Effects:** Rapid, overlapping notification chimes (bank app, calendar reminder, email ping) — deliberately grating, slightly too many, slightly too fast.

**Music Style:** A single tense, arrhythmic piano note repeated — no melody yet, just unresolved tension.

**Background:** A real wood desk texture, warm practical lighting from a desk lamp just out of frame, cold blue light from the laptop screen competing with it.

**Emotional Goal:** Quiet frustration. Not anger — fatigue. The specific tiredness of being asked to be your own financial analyst with none of the tools of one.

**Sound Effects:** Ambient room tone, a clock ticking faintly, the coffee cup set down with a soft ceramic clink. All notification chimes from Scene 2 have stopped — deliberate silence now.

**Music Style:** The tense piano note from Scene 2 resolves downward into a minor, unresolved chord and holds — like a breath not yet released.

**Sound Effects:** Four soft distinct chimes, one per icon appearing, rising in pitch (do-mi-sol-do arpeggio feel). A low whoosh as they converge.

## SCENE 13
**Duration:** 3:25–3:50 (25s)

**Narration:**
> "A salary raise. A marriage. A house. A new child. Paying off a loan early. Retirement itself. Seventeen real life events — and every single one updates your entire plan, live, the moment it happens."

**Visual Description:** A fast, rhythmic montage — six quick vignettes, roughly 3–4 seconds each, cut to the beat: (1) a payslip notification with a higher number, dashboard goal-probability ring visibly ticking upward; (2) two wedding rings on a table, a "Household" merge animation on the Family screen; (3) a house-key handoff, a new "Home Loan" liability card sliding into the liabilities list; (4) a small pair of baby shoes, a new "Education Fund" goal card appearing; (5) a "Loan Paid Off" stamp animation, the liabilities list shrinking by one row with a satisfying collapse; (6) a calendar flipping forward to a retirement-age milestone, the Dashboard's retirement goal ring settling at its final, confident value. **Camera Direction:** Fast, confident cuts — no vignette held longer than 4 seconds. Each vignette uses a slightly different camera treatment (handheld-feeling push, static product shot, top-down flat-lay, etc.) to keep the rapid montage visually varied rather than repetitive.

**Animation:** Each vignette's UI counterpart animates its specific change (a number ticking up, a card sliding in, a card collapsing out) at a snappy, satisfying pace (300–500ms per micro-animation) — this is the film's most kinetic sequence.

**Background:** Alternates between real-world vignette environments (warm, human, lived-in) and the navy product UI — the contrast is intentional: life happens in the world, the plan updates in the system.

**Transitions:** Hard, rhythmic cuts between all six vignettes (no dissolves — this section is about momentum). Hard cut out to Scene 14.

**On-Screen Text:** A single small label per vignette, appearing bottom-left for exactly the vignette's duration: "Salary Raise" / "Marriage" / "House Purchase" / "Birth of a Child" / "Loan Payoff" / "Retirement."

**Emotional Goal:** Momentum and delight. The specific pleasure of watching a system keep up with you instead of the reverse.

**Sound Effects:** A distinct, satisfying "confirm" sound per vignette (six variations on one sonic motif, so they feel related but not identical) — payslip chime, ring-tap, key-jingle, soft coo, stamp-thud, clock-chime.

**Music Style:** The doubled-tempo rhythm section from Scene 12 is now fully audible — energetic but still melodic, never chaotic. Each vignette's sound effect lands precisely on a musical downbeat.

## ANIMATION NOTES (global)

- **Recurring pulse motif:** the single "breathing" light pulse introduced in Scene 1 recurs deliberately in Scenes 8, 18, 22, and 26 — each recurrence should use the identical pulse timing (2-second breathing cycle) so an attentive viewer recognizes it as the film's visual heartbeat, resolving from anxious (S1) to systemic (S8, S18) to complete (S26).
- **Two distinct animation languages:** Acts 1–6 use clean, precise, product-consistent motion design (ease-in-out-cubic curves, confident snaps, no overshoot/bounce). Act 7 deliberately switches to a rougher, hand-sketched animation language (visible line-draw imperfection, pencil-texture reveals) — this distinction is load-bearing for the "shipped vs. vision" separation and must not be softened in execution.
- **Product UI must be real:** every UI capture (Scenes 9, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 20) should be sourced from or faithfully match actual Northstar product screens — Deep Navy Premium palette, real component styling — not generic dashboard stock templates.
- **Counting/ticking animations** (probability rings, test-pass counters) should always ease-out (fast start, slow settle) — this reads as "confident arrival at a real number," not "random number generator."

## MUSIC SUGGESTIONS

A single continuous score (not stock-music stitching), structured in five movements matching the emotional arc:

1. **Movement I — Tension (Act 1):** sparse solo piano, unresolved minor intervals, near-silence between phrases. Reference feel: minimalist, anxious restraint (in the sonic territory of Nils Frahm's quieter solo-piano work).
2. **Movement II — Arrival (Act 2–3):** the tension resolves into a warm major-key theme, piano and strings, joined gradually by soft mallet percussion — steady, confident, unhurried build. Reference feel: the calm-confidence register of Ólafur Arnalds' more orchestral pieces.
3. **Movement III — Momentum (Act 4):** tempo doubles, rhythm section becomes prominent, melody remains legible throughout — energetic without becoming chaotic. Reference feel: a controlled, melodic build-montage cue, not a generic "epic trailer" hit.
4. **Movement IV — Precision (Act 5–6):** returns to a controlled tempo, introduces a more mechanical/precise rhythmic layer under the same melodic theme — communicates engineering rigor without losing warmth.
5. **Movement V — Openness → Resolution (Act 7–8):** theme fragments into a sparser, more exploratory variation for Act 7 (intentionally unresolved), then returns in full, complete resolution for Act 8's closing statement — the fullest, warmest statement of the main theme in the entire film.

**Tempo arc:** ~62 BPM (Act 1) → ~78 BPM (Act 2–3) → ~118 BPM (Act 4) → ~92 BPM (Act 5–6) → ~70 BPM (Act 7) → ~80 BPM, full resolution (Act 8).

## CAMERA DIRECTION GLOSSARY (as used throughout)

- **Static/locked-off:** camera does not move; used for title cards and moments requiring stillness (Scenes 1, 4, 12, 15, 16, 26).
- **Push-in:** slow forward camera movement toward the subject, building intimacy/focus (Scenes 5, 9-adjacent framing, 14, 21, 25).
- **Pull-back reveal:** camera moves backward from a tight subject to reveal scale/context (Scene 22).
- **Orbital drift:** camera arcs around a central subject at a shallow angle, suggesting inspection/completeness (Scenes 8, 18).
- **Continuous dolly/pan:** camera moves laterally without cutting, reinforcing "one continuous system" (Scenes 10, 19→20, 24).
- **Hard cut montage:** rapid, rhythmic cuts with no camera movement within each shot, used for kinetic energy (Scene 13).
